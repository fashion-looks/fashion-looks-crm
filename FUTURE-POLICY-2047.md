# Fashion Looks™ × Amit Singh — Future Policy 2047

**Policy version:** 1.0  
**Created:** 2026-10-11  
**Review cadence:** Quarterly, and after any major security or business change  
**Owner:** Fashion Looks™ project owner

## 1. Mission

Build a lawful, resilient, technology-driven business and personal operating system that combines textile expertise, sustainability, AI, automation and disciplined execution. Grow Fashion Looks™ by delivering trustworthy client service, improving repeatable processes, learning continuously, and making decisions from verified data.

This is a direction and governance policy—not a guarantee of wealth, income, health, or any particular outcome.

## 2. North-star principles

1. **Integrity first:** Be truthful with clients, partners, authorities and users. Never fabricate certification status, credentials, revenue or results.
2. **Protect data:** Collect the minimum information needed; explain why it is needed; respect confidentiality and applicable law.
3. **Control access:** Use least privilege, multi-factor authentication, strong unique passwords and separate recovery methods.
4. **Verify everything:** Validate important data, AI outputs, invoices, certificate details, permissions and deployments before acting.
5. **Human approval for high-impact actions:** AI may organize, summarize, recommend and draft. It must not independently send external messages, move money, change critical access, delete important data or make binding business commitments.
6. **Automation with oversight:** Automate repetitive low-risk work; log actions, provide a pause/disable mechanism and keep a manual fallback.
7. **Build for the long term:** Prefer sustainable client value, sound records, maintainable software and steady learning over shortcuts or vanity metrics.
8. **Personal balance:** Protect time for rest, movement, relationships and reflection alongside business goals.

## 4. Personal execution policy

### Daily
- Select up to three important priorities.
- Complete the highest-value next action before adding unnecessary complexity.
- Follow up with clients respectfully and record the next action.
- Record actual receipts separately from estimates or proposals.
- Capture one lesson and set tomorrow's first action.
- Make space for movement, rest and personal responsibilities.

### Weekly
- Review completed work versus planned work.
- Review overdue client actions, pending documents and unpaid invoices.
- Check actual income and expenses from reliable records.
- Identify one process improvement and one learning goal.
- Back up important records and verify that the backup can be restored.

### Monthly / quarterly
- Review business performance, customer feedback, compliance responsibilities and operating costs.
- Review user access, connected apps, API keys and deployment permissions.
- Test backup restoration and incident recovery.
- Update this policy with a dated change note.

## 5. Data security and privacy

- Never commit passwords, API keys, private keys, recovery codes, identity documents, banking credentials or confidential customer data to GitHub.
- Store secrets in approved secret managers or deployment secrets; rotate exposed or unused keys.
- Use least-privilege access and MFA for GitHub, Cloudflare, domain registrar, email and business-critical accounts.
- Keep personal and business data separated where practical.
- Use encrypted backups where appropriate, maintain more than one protected copy, and test recovery.
- Keep audit logs for important access and administrative changes without collecting unnecessary personal data.
- Define retention periods and delete data when there is no lawful or operational need to keep it.
- Do not assume a public repository, browser local storage or a prototype dashboard is a secure cloud database.
- Before connecting a new service, check permissions, privacy terms, data location, export options and how access can be revoked.

## 6. AI governance

AI systems must:
- State uncertainty and distinguish facts from assumptions.
- Use only context needed for the task.
- Avoid inventing client records, certificate status, financial numbers or external actions.
- Present recommendations for human review before consequential decisions.
- Never request passwords, authentication codes or payment credentials.
- Provide a way to review, export and delete stored user data when feasible.
- Fail safely: if a service is unavailable, preserve user work and explain what did not happen.
- Keep API keys on the server; never expose them in frontend JavaScript or public repositories.
- Use authentication, rate limits, usage limits and monitoring before exposing an AI endpoint publicly.

## 7. Business integrity and certification work

- Describe services accurately and avoid guaranteeing certification, approval or issuance timelines.
- Follow the relevant certification body's procedures and requirements.
- Maintain clear document status, ownership, due dates and client communication records.
- Keep proposals, estimated fees, invoices and actual receipts distinct.
- Protect factory, supplier, buyer and employee information.
- Escalate unclear requirements rather than guessing or misrepresenting compliance.

## 8. Financial decision rules

- Use verified records and realistic cash-flow assumptions.
- Separate revenue, profit, cash received, taxes, liabilities and estimated pipeline value.
- Do not treat a target as guaranteed income or a forecast as a fact.
- Avoid decisions based only on AI output; validate material tax, legal and investment decisions with appropriate qualified professionals.
- Prioritize sustainable operations and adequate reserves over unverified promises of rapid returns.

## 9. Software delivery and change control

- Use a branch and pull request for meaningful changes.
- Review code and test on staging before production deployment.
- Keep production credentials out of source control.
- Make backups before risky migrations or destructive changes.
- Document deployment, rollback and recovery steps.
- Require explicit human approval for production releases, destructive operations and permission changes.
- Keep a manual fallback for critical workflows.

## 10. Incident response

If an account or system may be compromised:
1. Pause affected automation and revoke suspicious sessions or tokens.
2. Rotate exposed credentials from a trusted device.
3. Preserve relevant logs and timestamps.
4. Restore from a verified clean backup if necessary.
5. Check domain/DNS, repository permissions, deployment changes and connected applications.
6. Notify affected parties or authorities when required by applicable law.
7. Document the cause and preventive changes after recovery.

## 11. Policy ownership and amendments

The project owner reviews this policy quarterly. Each amendment should record:
- Date and version
- What changed
- Why it changed
- Security/privacy impact
- Required approvals and follow-up actions

No policy or AI automation overrides applicable law, platform terms, professional obligations or a person's right to review important actions.
